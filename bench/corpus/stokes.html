Creeping Flow in a Lid-Driven Square Cavity

G. Fluid and H. Flow

1. Problem description

We consider the steady flow of an incompressible Newtonian fluid in the unit square cavity Ω=(0,1)×(0,1). The upper wall, y=1, slides in its own plane with a constant velocity, whereas the other three walls are at rest. The Reynolds number is so small that inertia is negligible.

2. Governing equations

The velocity u=(ux,uy) and the pressure p satisfy the Stokes equations

−∇·(2νε(u))+∇p=0in Ω,(1)
∇·u=0in Ω,(2)

where ν is the kinematic viscosity and ε(u)=12(∇u+∇uT) is the rate of strain tensor.

3. Boundary conditions

On the moving lid the velocity is prescribed,

u=ulidon y=1,(3)

and the remaining walls, x=0, x=1 and y=0, are no-slip walls, u=0. Because the velocity is prescribed on the whole boundary, the pressure is determined only up to an additive constant.

4. Parameters

The viscosity is ν=0.1 and the lid moves in the positive x-direction with ulid=(1,0).

5. Discussion

A primary vortex forms slightly above the centre of the cavity; secondary vortices appear in the lower corners only for much larger cavities.
